# The Turaev–Viro state sum

## Abstract (Alpha)

This API defines quantum integers and quantum 6j symbols at a root of unity `q = e^{iπ/r}` and the
Turaev–Viro state sum of a triangulated 3-manifold built from them. It proves that the normalised
state sum is invariant under the 2-3 and 1-4 Pachner moves, given the quantum Biedenharn–Elliott
and orthogonality identities, and proves both identities, and hence invariance outright, at
level `r = 3`.

## Content (Alpha)

At `q = e^{iπ/r}` with `r ≥ 3`, the quantum integers are positive in the range used by the model
[1], and the quantum 6j symbols have the tetrahedral symmetries [2]. The Turaev–Viro state sum
replaces integers by quantum integers and 6j symbols by quantum 6j symbols in the Ponzano–Regge
sum, cuts spins off at level `r − 2`, and normalises by one factor per vertex {1} [3].

A general state-sum model of this shape is invariant under the 2-3 move when its 6j symbols
satisfy the Biedenharn–Elliott identity, and under the 1-4 move when they also satisfy
orthogonality [4]. For the Turaev–Viro model this gives invariance under both moves, with the
quantum Biedenharn–Elliott and quantum orthogonality identities as explicit hypotheses [3]. At
`r = 3` both identities are proven, so the state sum is invariant without hypotheses [5]. The two
identities for `r ≥ 4` are not yet formalized. [TODO]

The q-Racah sum satisfies a three-term recurrence in the spins, proven at `q = e^{iπ/r}` for the
real finite sums `SR` under explicit conditions: some `a_i = L + 1` with `L ≥ 0`,
`b₁ - a_i ≤ r - 2`, `b₂ - L ≤ r - 2`, `b₃ - L ≤ r - 2`, and `r ∤ b₂ + b₃ - a₂ - a₃ + k` for
`k = 0, 1, 2` [6], obtained by telescoping [7] from the steps at the root of unity [8], with
the denominators in closed form [9]. The case where all `a_i = 0` and the case where `r` divides
one of those quantum-integer arguments are excluded; the second occurs for admissible data. The
recurrence is the input from which orthogonality and the general Biedenharn–Elliott identity are
to follow.

For the 6j symbol the recurrence in the third colour is made explicit with closed-form
coefficients [11], [12], vanishing of the sum just outside the admissible range [12], and the
row identity `sixj_row` for admissible data away from `x = 0` and `x = r - 2` [13]. The
coefficients are symmetric for the weight `jOmega` (detailed balance, [14]). An abstract
weighted Jacobi lemma [10] then gives orthogonality of the sums for different sixth colours,
since the eigenvalues `2 cos((q+1)π/r)` are distinct [15]. Exchanging the third and sixth colour
(permuting the `α` arguments of the sum) gives the same orthogonality in the sixth colour [16]. At the lowest colour the sum has a single
nonzero term [17], and a Gram-matrix argument [18] then shows that the weighted norms of all rows
and of all columns of the matrix of sums are one common constant [19]. The value of that constant
(the boundary evaluation, a terminating very-well-poised sum) is not proved; it is the hypothesis
`QDougall`, and under it every norm is `1` [20]. The common admissible colours of two triads form a progression [21];
orthogonality in the model's form follows from `QDougall` [22]. The two endpoint rows of the
recurrence, at the third colours `0` and `r - 2`, are proved separately [24], [25], [26] and packaged with the
general coefficients into one row identity [27], so no colour needs special treatment, and `qOrthogonality` follows from `QDougall` alone [23].

The boundary evaluation `QDougall` is a terminating very-well-poised 6φ5 summation (the q-Dougall
summation); it is checked numerically (exact at `r = 4` to `7`) but not proved in Lean, so the
results that use it are conditional, and no instance of the hypothesis is proved here. The
quantum Biedenharn–Elliott identity for `r ≥ 4` is not formalized either. Several polynomial
identities of the recurrence are certificates checked by `decide +kernel` (the modules
`RacahCert*`, `RacahTreeX0` and their relatives, generated by `scripts/qracah/`), which adds no
axiom, and none of these modules uses `maxHeartbeats 0`.
