# Spacetime geometry in coordinates

## Abstract (Alpha)

This API describes spacetime geometry the way general relativity textbooks compute it: a metric is
given by its components in a chart, and the Christoffel symbols, the Riemann and Ricci tensors and
the scalar curvature are the standard coordinate formulas. It also defines Lorentzian metrics on
smooth manifolds and connects them to their coordinate components, so that curvature statements
proven in coordinates apply to the components of a manifold-level metric.

## Content (Alpha)

A coordinate metric on an open set is a smooth, symmetric, invertible matrix-valued function of
the coordinates [1]. From it one computes the Christoffel symbols, the Riemann tensor, the Ricci
tensor and the scalar curvature by the standard formulas {1} [1]. Minkowski space is flat [1], and the standard basis of `ℝⁿ` is a Lorentz frame for the Minkowski form [6].

The lowered Riemann tensor is symmetric under exchange of its index pairs, antisymmetric in its last pair, and satisfies the first
Bianchi identity [2]. The second Bianchi identity in coordinates is not yet formalized. [TODO]

Under a smooth change of coordinates the Riemann tensor, the Ricci tensor and the scalar curvature
transform as tensors, and solutions of the vacuum Einstein equations pull back to solutions, for any cosmological constant [3].

A Lorentzian metric on a smooth manifold modelled on `ℝⁿ` is a smooth symmetric bilinear form on
each tangent space with signature (−,+,…,+), in the mostly-plus convention {2} [4]. In every chart
of the atlas its components form a coordinate metric of Lorentzian signature [5].

On a manifold modelled on a normed space the Lie bracket of vector fields is the commutator of derivations of smooth functions, `[X, Y] g = X (Y g) - Y (X g)` [9]; Mathlib has this for a vector space only, and the identity is the step for the tensoriality of the curvature below.

A Lorentzian metric has a Levi-Civita connection, the torsion-free connection compatible with it, given by the Koszul formula, and any two agree on vector fields differentiable at a point [10].

In a chart of a manifold modelled on `ℝⁿ` the coordinate vector fields commute and the Levi-Civita connection is given by the Christoffel symbols of the coordinate components, `∇_{∂ᵢ} ∂ⱼ = Γᵏᵢⱼ ∂ₖ` [11].

For a smooth connection on a Hausdorff manifold modelled on a finite-dimensional space without boundary, the curvature defined with extensions of tangent vectors does not depend on the extensions: in a chart it is an expression in the Christoffel tensor of the connection and its derivative, a function of the values of the three vectors only [12], [13].

For a Levi-Civita connection of a smooth Lorentzian metric (same hypotheses) the first Bianchi identity holds, the lowered curvature is antisymmetric in each pair of slots and symmetric under exchange of the pairs, and the Ricci tensor is symmetric [14]. For any model with corners (boundary and corners allowed, no Hausdorff hypothesis) the same holds for a locally smooth connection that is torsion free and compatible with the metric, and the curvature is computed by any fields smooth near the point [131]; the covariant derivative of the Levi-Civita connection is smooth there [132]. The closed unit segment is an example with boundary: the identities hold at its boundary points [133]. The line with two origins is a non-Hausdorff example on which the identities also hold [134].

In a chart of a manifold modelled on `ℝⁿ` (Hausdorff), the components of the curvature of a Levi-Civita connection are the coordinate Riemann tensor, its Ricci tensor and scalar curvature are the coordinate ones, and the Einstein equations hold on the manifold iff the coordinate vacuum equations hold in every chart [15]; for any model with corners the same agreement holds for the Riemann, Ricci and scalar curvature of a Levi-Civita connection [128]. The divergence of the Einstein tensor vanishes on a manifold with corners modelled on any finite-dimensional space, by transport of the theorem for Fin n to R along a linear equivalence [135]. In the charts of the transported model, the Riemann tensor, the Ricci tensor, the scalar curvature and the Einstein equations agree with the coordinate ones on a manifold with corners modelled on any finite-dimensional space [151]. A metric of any signature has constant curvature `K` when `R(X, Y) Z = K (g(Y, Z) X - g(X, Z) Y)` for every locally smooth Levi-Civita connection; this follows from the coordinate identity in every chart, Minkowski space has constant curvature `0`, and a maximally symmetric metric has in addition `n (n + 1) / 2` independent Killing fields [152]. The pullback of a symmetric bilinear form of any signature by an immersion is a metric when it is nondegenerate on every tangent space, which holds on the tangent spaces of a quadric `L(x, x) = c`, `c ≠ 0` [153]. A quadric with the induced metric has constant curvature `1/c`, by the Gauss formula on the manifold [156]. The round 2-sphere of radius r (Mathlib's unit sphere with the metric r² h, modelled on Fin 2 → ℝ) has scalar curvature 2 / r² at every point for the intrinsic scalar curvature of every locally smooth Levi-Civita connection, through the conformal factor of the stereographic charts [138], [139], [140], [141].

For a smooth torsion-free connection the second Bianchi identity holds: the cyclic sum of the covariant derivative of the curvature vanishes [16].

In a chart, the covariant derivative of a bilinear form field, in particular of the Einstein tensor, is given by the components formula, so it is tensorial in the three arguments [17].

For a coordinate metric the contracted Bianchi identity holds, `g^{στ} ∇_σ G_{τν} = 0` for the coordinate Einstein tensor [18], and for the Levi-Civita connection of a Lorentzian metric on a manifold the divergence of the Einstein tensor vanishes [19]; the Einstein equations with a cosmological constant and a source, and in dimension other than two the vacuum equations are `Ric = 0` [23].

The Einstein equations with a source are the coordinate equations in every chart and on an open subset of the model space [77] [79]; and the FLRW metric on the manifold layer solves Einstein's equations with a perfect fluid iff Friedmann's equations hold [78].

A Lorentzian coordinate metric on an open subset of `ℝⁿ` is a Lorentzian metric on that open subset as a manifold, with the same components in every chart [20]; exterior Schwarzschild on the manifold layer satisfies the vacuum Einstein equations and has vanishing Ricci tensor [21]; the Levi-Civita connection of a Lorentzian metric is a smooth covariant derivative [22].
The volume measure of a Lorentzian metric on a manifold is the pushforward of `√|det g| dx` in each chart, and for a set in a chart domain it is given by the integral over its chart image in any chart [24].

For a nondegenerate metric of any signature, integration over a chart domain is the integral of `√|det g|` times the function in the chart; the volume measure is locally finite, finite on a compact manifold and positive on nonempty open sets [142]. The pairing g⁻¹(df, dh) of two differentials is the same in every chart [143], and the inverse metric and the density are smooth on a chart target [144]. The Laplace–Beltrami operator `Δf = -|g|^{-1/2} ∂ᵢ(|g|^{1/2} g^{ij} ∂ⱼ f)` (sign of the nonnegative Laplacian) is defined in a chart [145], has the weak form `∫ ψ Δf = ∫ g⁻¹(dψ, df)` and does not depend on the chart [146]; on a compact manifold Green's identity and the symmetry `∫ h Δf = ∫ f Δh` hold [147], and for a Riemannian metric the operator is nonnegative [148]. On Minkowski space `Δ(yᵢ²) = -2ηᵢᵢ` [149]. On an open set of `ℝⁿ` the coordinate operator obeys the product rule and the chain rule [150].

The coordinate components [1] and the volume measure are defined for a smooth symmetric nondegenerate bilinear field on the tangent bundle, a metric of any signature, of which a Lorentzian metric and a smooth Riemannian metric of Mathlib are the two cases [4]; the coordinate matrix of a Riemannian metric is positive definite [25]. On an open subset of Euclidean space, a coordinate metric of any signature is a metric of the open subset with the same components in every chart, and the volume of a measurable set is the integral of the square root of the absolute value of the determinant over its image [26].

The Friedmann equations on a product of an open interval and the 3-sphere: the metric is the pullback of a bilinear form by a smooth embedding [93] (the round metric of the sphere is the case of the inclusion [94]); in the stereographic charts the round metric is conformal to the Euclidean one [95], the product model is moved to four coordinates [96], the FLRW metric is Lorentzian there [97] with the coordinate FLRW components [98], and the Einstein equations with a source reduce to the chart equations [99], hence to Friedmann's equations [100].

On a manifold with corners (any model with corners, boundary included) the coordinate curvature of a metric in a chart is the coordinate formula with derivatives within the range of the model [90]; its symmetries use that mixed partial derivatives within that set commute [91], and the contracted Bianchi identity and the divergence-free Einstein tensor hold for these formulas [92]. On such a manifold modelled on `Fin n → ℝ`, the Einstein tensor of the Levi-Civita connection of a nondegenerate metric is divergence free, through the covariant derivative of a bilinear field in components [108]. The second Bianchi identity holds for a locally smooth torsion-free connection on a manifold modelled with corners [109]. The chart layer for any model with corners that the Friedmann equations on `J × S³` use is in `Lightlike/Spacetime/Corners/` (the names the boundaryless layer owns carry a `W`); for the boundaryless model it agrees with the boundaryless layer, the coordinate metric included [101].

A Lorentzian metric extends this structure, and the Levi-Civita connection, the curvature symmetries, the second Bianchi identity, the divergence of the Einstein tensor and the Einstein equations are stated and proved for it, so they hold for every metric of every signature [7].

The Lie derivative of the metric along a vector field, `(L_X g)(V, W) = X(g(V, W)) - g([X, V], W) - g(V, [X, W])`, equals `g(∇_V X, W) + g(V, ∇_W X)` for the Levi-Civita connection (metric compatibility and vanishing torsion), so a field is a Killing field, `g(∇_v X, w) + g(v, ∇_w X) = 0`, exactly when `L_X g = 0` [27]. The Lie derivative of a covector field and of a covariant 2-tensor field is defined the same way, from the Lie bracket, with `lieDerivMetric` as its value on the metric [136]. The bracket of two Killing fields is Killing, so the Killing fields form a Lie algebra [28]; sums and real multiples of Killing fields are Killing, which makes the smooth Killing fields a real Lie algebra with the Jacobi identity of the bracket [137]. In Minkowski space the affine fields `y -> A y + c` with `A` skew for the metric are Killing, the dilation is not, and their brackets are again of this form with the Poincare relations [29]. On any manifold with corners whose metric is induced by a smooth map `ι` into a space with a symmetric bilinear form `L`, a field `ι`-related to an `L`-skew linear field `x ↦ A x` has `L_X g = 0` and is Killing, by the naturality of the Lie bracket along `ι`; the boost of 1+1 Minkowski space is an example [154]. Such a field is smooth wherever `ι` is an immersion and `dι X` is smooth, which makes the restrictions of ambient linear fields to a quadric smooth vector fields [155]. De Sitter space is the whole manifold `ℝ × Sᵐ` with the metric induced from the hyperboloid, Lorentzian at every point [157]; the Lorentz algebra of the embedding space gives its `n (n + 1) / 2` independent Killing fields [158], and with constant curvature `1/L²` it is maximally symmetric [159]. The Poincaré ball, whose metric has the components `hyperbolicMetric`, is maximally symmetric with constant curvature `-1` on the whole ball [160]. Anti-de Sitter space is the whole manifold `S¹ × ℝᵐ` with the metric induced from the quadric, Lorentzian at every point [161]; the rotations and boosts of the embedding space of signature `(2, m)` give its `n (n + 1) / 2` independent Killing fields [162], and with constant curvature `-1/L²` it is maximally symmetric, on the quadric and not on its universal cover [163]. Every `C²` Killing field of Minkowski space is of this form, so the Killing fields form a vector space of dimension `n (n + 1) / 2` [61]. Conversely, the generator `X = ∂_sΦ(0, ·)` of a `C²` one-parameter family `Φ(s, ·)` of maps preserving the metric satisfies Killing's equation `DG(X)(u, w) + G(DX u, w) + G(u, DX w) = 0` in coordinates [44]. A coordinate field along which the metric components are constant is a Killing field of the Levi-Civita connection [41]; so the time translation `∂_t` is a Killing field of exterior Schwarzschild [42]. On a manifold a field is conformal Killing when `L_X g = 2σ g`; Killing fields are conformal Killing (`σ = 0`) and conformal Killing fields form a vector space [89]. A field is conformal Killing for a flat metric `B` when `B(DX u, w) + B(u, DX w) = 2σ B(u, w)`: Killing fields, the homothety (`σ = 1`) and the special conformal fields `B(y, y) b - 2B(b, y) y` (`σ = -2B(b, y)`) are conformal Killing, and conformal Killing fields form a vector space; in dimension at least three every `C³` conformal Killing field is a translation plus a conformal linear map plus a special conformal field [88]. In coordinates, a covector field satisfies the Ricci identity `∇_a ∇_b θ_c - ∇_b ∇_a θ_c = -Rᵈ_{cab} θ_d`, and for a Killing field `∇_a ∇_b θ_c = Rᵈ_{abc} θ_d`: its second derivatives are determined by its value; so on a connected open set a Killing field is determined by its value and first derivatives at one point, since along every segment they solve a linear system of ordinary differential equations [103] [104]. On a connected manifold, a smooth Killing field of the Levi-Civita connection that vanishes with its covariant derivative at one point vanishes everywhere, so the Killing fields form a vector space of dimension at most `n (n + 1) / 2` [105]. In coordinates, Killing's equation `∇_a θ_b + ∇_b θ_a = 0` for the lowered covector `θ_b = g_{bc} Yᶜ` is the Lie-derivative form `Yᶜ ∂_c g_{ab} + ∂_a Yˡ g_{lb} + g_{al} ∂_b Yˡ = 0`; the Lie derivative of the bracket of two fields is the commutator of their Lie derivatives, so the bracket of two Killing fields is Killing [106]. On an open set of `ℝ³`, for a family of Killing fields normalized at a point like the space form's (transvections with `Pₘ(p) = eₘ` and `∇Pₘ(p) = 0`, rotations vanishing at `p` with `∇R_ab(p)` the generators of `so(3)`), the curvature at `p` is isotropic (the Leibniz identity of the brackets read through the data at `p`, and Schur's lemma: an `so(3)`-equivariant map of antisymmetric arrays is a multiple of the identity), so the family has the brackets of the space form of some curvature `k` on a connected open set, and on a ball the metric is, in orbit charts, a constant multiple of that space form [107].

For a non-null hypersurface given as a level set of a function, with unit normal `n`, the extrinsic curvature `K(v, w) = g(∇_v n, w)` is symmetric on tangent vectors, the Levi-Civita connection splits on tangent fields as `∇_Y Z = D_Y Z - ε K(Y, Z) n`, and the tangential and normal components of the curvature are the Gauss relation `g(R(X, Y) Z, W) = g(R^D(X, Y) Z, W) - ε (K(Y, Z) K(X, W) - K(X, Z) K(Y, W))` and the Codazzi relation `g(R(X, Y) Z, n) = -((D_X K)(Y, Z) - (D_Y K)(X, Z))` [30]. Contracted in an orthonormal basis `(n, E_a)` of the tangent space, `g(E_a, E_b) = η_a δ_ab`, they give the constraints: `2 G(n, n) = -ε R^D + K² - K_ab K^ab` (Hamiltonian) and `G(n, E_c) = Σ_a η_a ((D_{E_a} K)(E_c, E_a) - (D_{E_c} K)(E_a, E_a))` (momentum), with `G` the Einstein tensor [47]. The unit normal need not be assumed: a function whose gradient at a point is not null has level sets with a smooth unit normal near that point [64]. As a check, the spheres `‖y‖ = r` in a Euclidean space have second fundamental form `⟪v, w⟫ / r` and, by the Gauss relation, constant sectional curvature `1/r²`, with scalar curvature `2/r²` in `ℝ³` [63]. The constant-time slices of FLRW are such hypersurfaces, with unit normal `∂_t` and `K = (ȧ/a) h` [80].

The first variation of the Einstein-Hilbert density in coordinates: for a smooth metric `g` and a smooth symmetric perturbation `h` on `R^n`, the metric `g + t h` has volume density with derivative `1/2 sqrt(|g|) g^{ab} h_{ab}` [31], Christoffel symbols with the derivative `delta Gamma` [32], and curvature with first variation given by the linearised Riemann tensor [35] [36]; the contraction `g^{sigma nu} delta R_{sigma nu}` times the volume density is the divergence of `sqrt(|g|) W^rho` with `W^rho = g^{sigma nu} delta Gamma^rho_{sigma nu} - g^{rho sigma} delta Gamma^kappa_{kappa sigma}` (the Palatini identity [33] [34] [37]), so that the density `sqrt(|g|) (R - 2 Lambda)` has the first variation `-sqrt(|g|) (R^{ab} - R g^{ab}/2 + Lambda g^{ab}) h_{ab}` plus a divergence [37]. The divergence of a compactly supported field integrates to zero and the density is differentiable under the integral over a compact region, so the first variation of the integral over a compact region containing the support of the perturbation is `-∫ sqrt(|g|) (R^{ab} - R g^{ab}/2 + Lambda g^{ab}) h_{ab}`, with no boundary term [38]. A metric is a critical point of the Einstein-Hilbert action with a cosmological term, for every smooth symmetric perturbation of compact support, exactly when it satisfies the vacuum Einstein equations with that cosmological constant, on a manifold [129] and in coordinates, by the first variation and the fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations [39]. In two dimensions the Einstein tensor vanishes identically, so every metric is critical for the action without cosmological term [40].

A smooth metric of any signature on the product of the time line and three-dimensional space for which the three spatial translations and the three spatial rotations are Killing fields has `g_{0k} = 0` and `g_{ij}` a function of the time times `delta_{ij}`, the flat FLRW form [70]. For every spatial curvature `k`, the three rotations and the three curved translations `Pₘ = (1 - k|x|²/4)∂ₘ + (k/2)xₘ(x · ∂)` as Killing fields on the set `1 + k|x|²/4 > 0` give there the FLRW form `b(t) dt² + a(t) δ/(1 + k|x|²/4)²`, with the slices in isotropic coordinates [71]. The local flow of a Killing field preserves the metric [72]. Along the orbits of finitely many Killing fields whose brackets close with constant structure constants, the metric pulled back by the flow solves a linear equation in the structure constants alone, so two such families with the same structure constants and proportional Gram matrices at a point have proportional metrics along their orbits [73]: the first part of the step from abstract homogeneous and isotropic slices to these coordinates. On `ℝ³` the curved translations and the rotations are Killing for the space form `δ/(1 + k|x|²/4)²` and close with explicit structure constants [74]; so a metric with Killing fields of these structure constants whose rotations vanish at a point is, in the orbit chart `η ↦ exp(∑ ηₘ Pₘ)(p)`, a constant multiple of the space form in its own orbit chart [75]. If the rotations vanish along a worldline `c`, then in the chart `Φ(t, ξ) = exp(∑ ξₑ Yₑ)(c t)` the metric is `b(t) dt² + a(t) δ/(1 + k|x|²/4)²` with `b(t) = g(c', c')`: the cross terms vanish because the rotations fix the velocity of `c`, and the time-time component is constant along the orbits [76]. The focusing lemma for the Riccati inequality: a function on `0 ≤ s < L` with `Θ' ≤ -Θ²/m` (`m > 0`) and `Θ(0) < 0` has `L ≤ m/|Θ(0)|`, the analytic step of geodesic focusing from Raychaudhuri's equation [49].

Raychaudhuri's equation in coordinates: for a smooth field `v` geodesic on an open set of a coordinate metric, with `∇_b vᵃ = ∂_b vᵃ + Γᵃ_{bc} vᶜ` and the expansion `Θ = ∇ₐ vᵃ`, `v(Θ) = -(∇_b vᵃ)(∇ₐ vᵇ) - R_{σν} vᵠ vᵛ`; where `g(v, v) = -1`, splitting `∇v` into expansion, shear `σ` and rotation `ω` gives `v(Θ) = -Θ²/(n-1) - σ² + ω² - R(v, v)` [50] [51] [52]. For a unit geodesic field (`g(v, v) = -1` on the open set), along an integral curve for parameters `0 ≤ s < L` that starts converging, `R(v, v) + σ² - ω² ≥ 0` gives `L ≤ (n-1)/|Θ(0)|`; for a Lorentzian metric the shear term is nonnegative, so a rotation-free congruence with `R(v, v) ≥ 0` focuses [53] [54]. On a manifold, for the Levi-Civita connection and a smooth vector field `V` geodesic near `x`, `V(Θ) = -tr((∇V)²) - Ric(V, V)` with `Θ = tr(∇V)`, proved in the chart at `x` from the coordinate identity [55]. For a Lorentzian metric and a unit geodesic `V`, rotation-free (`g(∇_u V, w) = g(∇_w V, u)`) with `Ric(V, V) ≥ 0`, `V(Θ) ≤ -Θ²/(n-1)`, and along an integral curve of `V` for `0 ≤ s < L` with `Θ(γ(0)) < 0`, `L ≤ (n-1)/|Θ(γ(0))|` [55]. For a null geodesic field `k` with an auxiliary null `N` (`g(k, N) = -1`) and the screen metric `h = g + k♭ N♭ + N♭ k♭`, projecting `∇k` onto the screen gives `k(Θ) = -Θ²/(n-2) - σ̂² + ω̂² - R(k, k)` [58] [59]; for a Lorentzian metric `σ̂² ≥ 0`, so a null field with `ω̂ = 0` (true for a rotation-free one) and `R(k, k) ≥ 0` focuses within `(n-2)/|Θ(0)|`, in coordinates and on a manifold [59] [60]. The expansion `Θ = tr ∇k` of a null geodesic field is the frame expansion `Σᵢ g(∇_{eᵢ} k, eᵢ)` over `n - 2` orthonormal vectors orthogonal to the null pair `k`, `N`, because `g(∇_u k, k) = 0` for a null field and `∇_k k = 0`; this links the expansion of a null normal field along a surface to the `Θ` of the focusing theorem [110].

The null, weak, strong and dominant energy conditions are stated pointwise for a symmetric bilinear form on a Lorentzian vector space; dust with non-negative density satisfies all four, a positive cosmological constant violates the strong one, and a scalar field with negative potential at a point of vanishing gradient violates the weak one [43]. On a Lorentzian 4-manifold the four conditions hold for a stress-energy field at every point; for a perfect fluid they take the effective forms, and under the Einstein equations they are equivalent to the geometric forms on the Ricci and Einstein tensors [45]. For a perfect fluid the tensor conditions are Physlib's effective conditions on a cosmic fluid at c = 1 [46]. The electromagnetic field satisfies the weak, dominant and strong conditions, through a Lorentz frame started by any unit timelike vector [48].

The conformally related metric `e^{2ψ} g` of a coordinate metric has the Christoffel symbols `Γᵃ_{bc} + δᵃ_c ∂_b ψ + δᵃ_b ∂_c ψ - g_{bc} gᵃᵈ ∂_d ψ`, and the Riemann tensor of any metric whose Christoffel symbols differ from those of `g` by a tensor `C` is that of `g` plus the derivative and quadratic terms in `C` [62]. In any dimension, the Ricci tensor of `e^{2ψ} g` is `R_{σν} - (n - 2) ∇_σ ∇_ν ψ - g_{σν} □ψ + (n - 2) ∂_σ ψ ∂_ν ψ - (n - 2) g_{σν} |dψ|²` and its scalar curvature is `e^{-2ψ} (R - 2 (n - 1) □ψ - (n - 1)(n - 2) |dψ|²)`, from the covariant derivative of the difference tensor, which is built from the covariant Hessian of `ψ` [67]. The Riemann tensor of `e^{2ψ} g` follows in the same way, and for `n ≥ 3` the Weyl tensor with all indices down scales by `e^{2ψ}` while the Weyl tensor with its first index raised is unchanged: the Weyl tensor is conformally invariant [68].

The gradient `ℓ` of a function has covariant derivative the Hessian, so `∇_ℓ ℓ = ½ grad (ℓ · ℓ)`: where the level sets are null hypersurfaces, `ℓ` is geodesic, and `ℓ' = e^ρ ℓ` satisfies `∇_{ℓ'} ℓ' = ⟨dρ, ℓ'⟩ ℓ'`, the null generators with a non-affine normalization, in coordinates [65]. On a manifold, the gradient `♯du` of a smooth function is a smooth vector field with these components in every chart, and if the level sets of `u` are null near a point, it is geodesic there for the Levi-Civita connection, and `ℓ' = f ♯du` satisfies `∇_{ℓ'} ℓ' = ⟨df, ♯du⟩ ℓ'` [66].

The source of the Einstein equations is conserved: if `G + Λ g = κ T` with `κ ≠ 0` for the Levi-Civita connection, the divergence of `T` vanishes, since the Einstein tensor is divergence free and the metric is parallel [69].

The divergence theorem on a manifold: for a metric of any signature, its Levi-Civita connection, a smooth vector field `X` vanishing outside a compact set and a measurable domain `D` given near every point of its closure by a defining function (`D = {u ≥ 0}`, `du ≠ 0` where `u = 0`), `∫_D div X d vol_g` is the flux of `X` through the boundary of `D`, written with a partition of unity subordinate to charts and graph charts of the boundary in each chart; a density `f · dV_g` is the case of the field `f X` [57]. In a chart, `√|g| ∇ₐXᵃ = ∂ⱼ(√|g| Xʲ)`, and the theorem in `ℝⁿ⁺¹` needs its hypothesis only on the support of the field [56] [57].

The first variation of the Christoffel symbols is a tensor: `δΓⁱ_{jk} = ½ gⁱˡ (∇ⱼ h_{lk} + ∇ₖ h_{lj} - ∇ₗ h_{jk})` with the covariant derivative of the perturbation for the Levi-Civita connection of `g`, and the boundary vector of the variation is `W^ρ = g^{ρl} g^{σν} (∇_σ h_{lν} - ∇_l h_{σν})`, the divergence of `h` minus the gradient of its trace [81]. For a symmetric tensor field `h` on a manifold, the covariant derivative `∇h` of the Levi-Civita connection has these components in every chart, and the boundary vector is a smooth vector field on the manifold, with components `W` in every chart, that vanishes wherever `h` vanishes near the point [82]. For a measurable region `D` and `h` vanishing outside a compact set `K`, the first variation of the Einstein–Hilbert action of `D ∩ K` is `∫_{D ∩ K} (-⟨G + Λ g, h⟩_g + div_g V(h)) d vol_g`, the bulk term plus the divergence of that vector field [83]. The Gibbons–Hawking–York density of a graph and the Palatini flux depend only on the germs of the metric and the perturbation at the boundary point, so the density of `g + t h` is affine in `t`, with slope minus the flux, for components given only near that point [84]. A function vanishing on a graph near a point, and differentiable there, has only a normal derivative at the point, so a graph on which a non-null defining function vanishes is not null; the graph is the frontier of the region above or below it, frontiers are local, and the chart preimage of a frontier point of the chart image of `D` is a frontier point of `D`; in a chart, `du(♯du) = Σ ∂ₐũ (G⁻¹)ᵃᵇ ∂_bũ` [85]. At a graph point where the chart image of a region is the graph region nearby, a perturbation vanishing on the boundary has components vanishing on the graph, and a nowhere null boundary makes the graph not null; the flux density of `w V(h)` in a chart is `(w ∘ φ⁻¹)` times the Palatini flux. So, for a measurable region `D` of a manifold with a nowhere null `C²` boundary and a perturbation `h` vanishing outside a compact set `K` and on `∂D`, the Einstein–Hilbert action of `D ∩ K` minus the Gibbons–Hawking–York term, in the graph charts of the divergence theorem for `V(h)`, has first variation `-∫_{D ∩ K} ⟨G + Λ g, h⟩_g d vol_g`: the boundary terms cancel [86].

Minkowski space is conformal to the region `0 < χ`, `|η| + χ < π` of the Einstein static universe: with `t = sin η / ω` and `r = sin χ / ω`, `ω = (cos η + cos χ)/2`, the metric `-dt² + dr² + r² dΩ²` is `ω⁻² (-dη² + dχ² + sin² χ dΩ²)`, and `(η, χ) ↦ (t, r)` maps that region bijectively onto `r > 0` [87]. A rescaling of the time direction only is conformal to the Einstein static universe form exactly when its factor squared is one [111]. On the closed region the conformal factor vanishes exactly on the boundary `|η| + χ = π`, conformal infinity [87]. In the null angles `U = (η + χ)/2`, `V = (η - χ)/2` one has `t = tan U + tan V`, `r = tan U - tan V`, and the dilation `(t, r) ↦ (λt, λr)` becomes the angle map `x ↦ arctan (λ tan x)`, which extends continuously by `±π/2`: the dilation extends to a continuous map of the closed region that acts as the dilation on the region and maps conformal infinity to itself [126].

Anti-de Sitter space is conformal to half of the Einstein static universe: with `r = tan θ`, the global metric `-(1 + r²) dτ² + (1 + r²)⁻¹ dr² + r² dΩ²` is `cos⁻² θ (-dτ² + dθ² + sin² θ dΩ²)` for `0 ≤ θ < π/2`, and the conformal boundary `θ = π/2`, where `cos θ` vanishes, carries the timelike metric `-dτ² + dΩ²` [102].
