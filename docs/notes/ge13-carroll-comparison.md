# GE12/GE13: comparison with Carroll (read 18 November 2026)

Source read: S. M. Carroll, *Lecture Notes on General Relativity* (1997), arXiv gr-qc/9712019, pdf text, ch. 5 and ch. 3; page numbers of the arXiv pdf.

## Killing fields and the Lie derivative (GE12)
* Killing field: `£_V g_{μν} = 0`, eq. (5.41), and equivalently `∇_{(μ} V_{ν)} = 0`, eq. (5.42) ("Killing's equation"), p. 140. The expression of the Lie derivative of the
  metric as `2 ∇_{(μ} V_{ν)}` is eq. (5.33) (cited by Carroll at (5.36), p. 138). Our `lieDerivMetric` and `LorentzianMetric.isKillingAt_iff` state the same
  two conditions as equivalent, at a point, for the Levi-Civita connection: match, with the difference that ours is a pointwise statement on smooth fields (no flow, no
  one-parameter family of isometries).
* Carroll's definition of a Killing field is through isometries generated by the field; ours is the Killing equation (`£_V g = 0`). He passes from one to the other with (5.33); ours
  does not prove that the flow consists of isometries (not attempted): comparison with Carroll: statement of the Killing equation matches, the flow characterisation is not covered.
* The Poincare example (affine fields with skew linear part): Carroll counts `n(n+1)/2` independent Killing vectors for maximally symmetric spaces (p. 140, around (5.4x)); we prove that
  the skew affine fields are Killing and close under the bracket, not that they are all of them.
  Update (18 November 2026 ~23:40, `KillingMinkowski.lean`): now also proved that they are all of them and the count. Carroll pp. 140-141, eq. (5.44): "a maximally
  symmetric space is one which possesses the largest possible number of Killing vectors, which on an n-dimensional manifold is n(n + 1)/2. We will not prove this
  statement"; for `ℝⁿ`, `n + n(n − 1)/2 = n(n + 1)/2` (translations and rotations); "the same kind of counting argument applies to ... Minkowski space".
  `killing_eq_affField`: every `C²` Killing field of Minkowski space is `y ↦ A y + c` with `A` `η`-skew; `finrank_killingFields`: the Killing fields form a space of
  dimension `n(n + 1)/2`. No difference: Carroll states the count for Minkowski space by analogy, we prove it (for `C²` fields, which includes his smooth ones).

## Gauss and Codazzi (GE13): source E. Gourgoulhon, *3+1 Formalism and Bases of Numerical Relativity*, arXiv gr-qc/0703035 (pdf text read)
* Extrinsic curvature `K(u, v) = −u · ∇_v n`, eq. (2.43), p. 24 of the pdf numbering used; Gauss relation `γ γ γ γ ⁴R^ρ_{σμν} = R^γ_{δαβ} + K^γ_α K_{δβ} − K^γ_β K_{αδ}`,
  eq. (2.92), p. 35; Codazzi relation `γ^γ_ρ n^σ γ γ ⁴R^ρ_{σμν} = D_β K^γ_α − D_α K^γ_β`, eq. (2.101), p. 36; contracted forms (2.93), (2.102), (2.103).
* Comparison with ours (`gauss_relation`, `codazzi_relation`, `secondFF`): our `K(v, w) = g(∇_v n, w)` is Gourgoulhon's `K` with the opposite sign. The quadratic term of the Gauss relation is
  independent of that sign: with `ε = g(n, n) = −1` ours reads `g(R(X,Y)Z, W) = g(R_D(X,Y)Z, W) + K(Y,Z)K(X,W) − K(X,Z)K(Y,W)`, and (2.92) read on `(X,Y,Z,W)` gives
  `R + K(X,W) K(Y,Z) − K(Y,W) K(X,Z)`: the same. For Codazzi, `g(R(X,Y)Z, n) = −g(R(X,Y)n, Z)` (antisymmetry in the first pair), and (2.101) gives
  `g(R(X,Y)n, W) = (D_Y K_G)(X,W) − (D_X K_G)(Y,W)`; with `K_G = −K` this is our `g(R(X,Y)Z, n) = −((D_X K)(Y,Z) − (D_Y K)(X,Z))`: the same.
* Differences: Gourgoulhon treats spacelike hypersurfaces (`g(n,n) = −1`) of a Lorentzian manifold; our statement is for every non-null hypersurface (`ε = ±1`); the case `ε = +1` is covered by the Ammann notes below. Gourgoulhon has the hypersurface as a manifold with its induced connection `D`; ours has the level sets of a function and the tangential connection `D`
  on tangent fields (leaves have no manifold structure). The symmetry of `K` is stated around eq. (2.43) ("is symmetric. It is called the second fundamental form", ch. 2.3) and in the remark after (2.75)-(2.76): read as stated, equation page not recorded; ours proves it (`secondFF_symm_fields`).

## Gauss and Codazzi for both signs: source M. Ammann (Regensburg), *Differential Geometry II, Lorentzian Geometry, Lecture Notes*, summer term 2021 (pdf text read)
* Setting: semi-Riemannian hypersurface, unit normal `ν` with `ε := g(ν, ν) ∈ {−1, +1}` (Def. 2.3.2, Thm. 2.3.7), shape operator `S(X) = −∇_X ν` (Def. 2.3.4), `g(S X, Y) = g(II(X,Y), ν)` (Lemma 2.3.5, p. 69).
* Gauss formula, Thm. 2.3.7 (i), p. 70: `R_M(X,Y)Z = (R̄(X,Y)Z)ᵀ + ε(⟨S Y, Z⟩ S X − ⟨S X, Z⟩ S Y)` (the bars on the curvatures were lost in the text extraction of the pdf; the reading is the standard one and the
  sectional-curvature form (ii) below it, `sec_M = sec̄ + ε det S/…`, fixes the sign of the `ε` term). In our notation (`K = −⟨S ·, ·⟩`, quadratic in `K`) this is
  `g(R̄(X,Y)Z, W) = g(R_D(X,Y)Z, W) − ε(K(Y,Z) K(X,W) − K(X,Z) K(Y,W))`, our `gauss_relation`, **for `ε = ±1`**.
* Codazzi, Thm. 2.3.11, p. 72: `π_N(R̄(X,Y)Z) = (∇_X II)(Y,Z) − (∇_Y II)(X,Z)` with `II(X,Y) = ε II_scalar(X,Y) ν` and `II_scalar = g(S ·, ·) = −K`: taking `g(·, ν)` gives
  `g(R̄(X,Y)Z, ν) = −((D_X K)(Y,Z) − (D_Y K)(X,Z))`, our `codazzi_relation`, for both signs.
* So the timelike-hypersurface case (`ε = +1`) is compared and matches; the earlier "pending" for `ε = +1` is closed by this source. Caveat: the bars in the pdf extraction were reconstructed from context.

## GE13 trust check: the sphere in a Euclidean space (`SphereHypersurface.lean`, 18 November 2026 ~23:55)
Source read: Gourgoulhon, gr-qc/0703035, Section 2.3, Example 3, pp. 27-28: the sphere of radius `R` in `ℝ³`, induced metric (2.52),
"ΣR = 2/R², R_ij = γ_ij/R², R^i_jkl = (δ^i_k γ_jl − δ^i_l γ_jk)/R²" (2.53), outward normal (2.54), `K_ij = −γ_ij/R` (2.58), `K = −2/R` (2.59).
Ours: in any finite-dimensional inner product space with the flat metric and the flat connection (proved Levi-Civita and smooth), the
spheres are the level sets of `‖y‖²` with unit normal `y/‖y‖` (`isUnitNormal_sphere`); `secondFF_sphere`: `K(v, w) = ⟪v, w⟫/r` on tangent
vectors; `gaussCurv_sphere`: `g(R^D(X, Y)Z, W) = (⟪Y, Z⟫⟪X, W⟫ − ⟪X, Z⟫⟪Y, W⟫)/r²` (from the Gauss relation, the ambient curvature being zero);
`scalarCurv_sphere`: in `ℝ³` the trace over an orthonormal tangent frame is `2/r²`.
Comparison: the Riemann tensor and the scalar curvature agree with (2.53). `K` has the opposite sign of (2.58): the sign convention of the
extrinsic curvature (ours `K(v, w) = g(∇_v n, w)`, Gourgoulhon's (2.43) `K(u, v) = −u · ∇_v n`), the convention already recorded for GE13 above.
As for the whole of GE13 the leaf has no manifold structure, so "scalar curvature" is the trace of the curvature `R^D` of the tangential
connection over an orthonormal tangent frame (the GE13 difference about the leaves, recorded above; no new difference).

## GE13 trust check: a constant-time slice of FLRW (`FLRWSlice.lean`, 19 November 2026 ~01:00)
Source read: Gourgoulhon, gr-qc/0703035, §3.3.5, eq. (3.24), p. 45: `L_m γ = −2N K` (with `m = N n`, (3.26) `L_n γ = N⁻¹ L_m γ`).
Ours: on `flrwOpen hI k` with `g = −dt² + a(t)² dx²/(1 + k|x|²/4)²`, the slices `t = const` are the level sets of `cosmicTime` with unit
normal `∂_t`, `ε = −1` (`isUnitNormal_flrwSlice`); for the Levi-Civita connection `K(v, w) = (ȧ/a)(g(v, w) + v⁰w⁰)`
(`secondFF_flrwSlice`), i.e. `K = (ȧ/a) h` with `h = g + n♭ ⊗ n♭`, and `K = (ȧ/a) g` on tangent vectors (`secondFF_flrwSlice_tangent`).
Comparison: in these coordinates the lapse is `N = 1`, the shift zero and `γ_ij = a² γ^(k)_ij`, so (3.24) gives `K = −½ ∂_t γ = −(ȧ/a) γ`;
ours is `+(ȧ/a) h`: the sign convention of the extrinsic curvature recorded for GE13 (ours `K(v, w) = g(∇_v n, w)`, Gourgoulhon (2.43)
`K(u, v) = −u · ∇_v n`). No new difference; the milestone check "a constant-time slice of FLRW has K = (ȧ/a) h" holds in our convention.
